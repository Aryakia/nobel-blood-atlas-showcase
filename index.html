Public Research Showcase

Nobel Blood Atlas

An evidence-graded research dashboard built around a simple principle: sparse data should look sparse. Missingness, conflicting claims, provenance, and documentation bias remain visible instead of being hidden behind a polished chart.

Research rules

Evidence before inference.

Blood type is never inferred from nationality, ancestry, personality, name, or appearance.
A laureate enters the dataset only when a source explicitly connects that person to a blood type.
Conflicting claims remain visible and are excluded from calculations until resolved.
Population comparisons are contextual and not biological conclusions from the current sparse sample.
What I built

Research dashboard + evidence ledger

Evidence grades

Records carry explicit evidence quality rather than being treated as equally reliable observations.

Contradiction tracking

Conflicting public claims stay visible for audit instead of being silently resolved.

Category filters

Explore Nobel categories while preserving the distinction between screened and corroborated records.

ABO comparisons

Observed-versus-expected views are presented with coverage and selection-bias warnings.

Research queue

Unknown, lead, conflicting, and unresolved records remain part of the visible research process.

Versioned data

Downloadable datasets and methodology notes support reproducibility and review.

Interpretation boundary: the current dataset is a screening dataset, not a complete medical dataset of Nobel laureates. Public disclosure differs substantially by country and language, and the observed records are not a representative sample.
Why this project

Data quality is part of the result.

The central design goal is to show how a research interface can make uncertainty, provenance, contradiction, selection bias, and missingness explicit rather than presenting incomplete evidence as certainty.
